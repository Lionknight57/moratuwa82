**Emeritus Professor P. A. de Silva**, a pioneering Sri Lankan mechanical
engineer, technologist and academic at the University of Moratuwa, has passed
away at the age of 92.

He was widely celebrated for **inventing Sri Lanka's first electric car** and for
**pioneering CAD/CAM technology** in the country.

He taught Thermodynamics in Final Part II for most of us.

He was last seen celebrating with [the batch that graduated in
1976](/gallery/our-teachers/).

## In his own words

The Department of Mechanical Engineering published
[an interview with him](https://uom.lk/mech/emeritus-professor-p-de-silva),
taken from *Bolgoda Chat* and conducted by Dr. Hirushie Karunathilake and
Dr. Sanjeeva Witharana. In it he describes building the electric vehicle in the
1970s — it passed 35 mph, over 95% of its parts were made in house, and he
believed it was the first car anywhere to use regenerative braking. He opened
the CAD/CAM centre at the university, chaired the Ceylon German Technical
Training Institute, and retired from the department in 2001, lecturing as a
visitor for years afterwards. He was still building prototypes, among them an
electric harvester and plough, and had written books in both Sinhala and English.

## A political chapter

His career was also marked by a significant political chapter. A staunch
supporter of the UNP's return to power in 1977, he served as **Chairman of the
Ceylon Steel Corporation from 1977 to 1980**, and subsequently of the **State
Hardware Corporation**. His tenure at the Steel Corporation was not without
controversy, and he became the subject of parliamentary scrutiny and criticism —
an unusual episode for an engineer and academic appointed to head a major state
corporation.

## The funeral

The remains lay at **Mahinda Florists, 591 Galle Road, Dehiwala–Mount Lavinia**,
from 2.00 p.m. on 14 August.

The funeral was held on **Saturday 15 August at 10.00 a.m.** at the **General
Cemetery (Nisala Sevana), Mount Lavinia**.

*May he attain the supreme bliss of Nibbāna.*
